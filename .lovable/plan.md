# The product after payment

The quiz, result page and checkout stay exactly as they are. Everything below is new, and it lives on the same design system so the funnel and the product feel like one thing.

## The one architectural rule

Today the plan is recomputed from quiz answers on every render. From now on the plan is **written once, at payment**, into its own record: starting book, translation, tradition, pace, purchase date, the 30 day rows. Everything after payment reads only that record. A buyer who never touched the quiz gets sensible defaults (John, WEB, no tradition split) and the product works identically.

## The buyer's path

1. The intro price is charged immediately at checkout — no free trial. "1-week access" is a label for the access period, not a Stripe trial; the subscription renews once that period ends.
2. The email already collected in the quiz becomes the account — passwordless.
3. Same tab, no download: they land on **My Plan**, day 1 open.
4. One email is sent: the charge confirmation plus a sign-in link.
5. Because magic links expire, `/auth` also carries a "send me a sign-in link" form — the emailed link is never the only way back in. A signed-out visitor hitting `/plan` lands on that form, never on an error.
6. Cancelling is one click inside the account. No email, no phone call — the paywall and FAQ already promise this.

## Screen 1 — My Plan

- Hero card: cropped public-domain artwork under a gradient, "Day 6 · John in 30 days" small uppercase, "Day 6" large. On white below: the session's real title (never a reference), one setup sentence, thin progress bar, near-black Continue.
- All 30 days listed: number, title, passage. Past = open and done, today = highlighted, future = visible but locked. Seeing all 30 is the proof the plan is real.
- Each day unlocks 24 hours after the previous one, counted from the payment timestamp, not by calendar date. Missing a week loses nothing.
- Progress reads "Day 6 of 30" plus finished sessions. Nothing can go down.
- After day 30 the screen isn't empty: it offers the other two books as a new plan.
- No menu, no tab bar.

## Screen 2 — The session

Full screen. Top bar: close, six segment bars, "3/6". One idea per step.

1. The passage — 4-8 verses, large serif, one word subtly underlined.
2. One thing that changes it — artwork card, insight stated as a claim, two or three sentences, hairline, initials circle and "Condensed from Matthew Henry, 1710 · Public domain". Exactly one.
3. What it meant then — two plain paragraphs, no card.
4. Where traditions differ — indigo card, only when the passage genuinely divides; each reading with its verses, one line of common ground, never a verdict. Otherwise the session is five steps.
5. One question — centred, spacious, optional text box.
6. Take it with you — mark done, what's next tomorrow. Quiet.

Position is saved per step, so reopening anywhere returns to the exact step.

## Content — generated once, stored

Three 30-day plans: John, Psalms, Mark. Ninety sessions, generated by a one-off build script and written to the database. Never generated on request.

- Commentary sources: Matthew Henry 1710, John Gill 1746, Albert Barnes 1834 — author and year shown every time.
- **Bible text: WEB, KJV or ASV only.** NIV, ESV, NLT, NASB, CSB, NABRE, NKJV and The Message never appear in code, database or any prompt.
- Verses are imported into a verses table from a public-domain source first; sessions reference verses, and any reference that doesn't resolve is dropped before the session is stored. No model-written scripture.
- Quiz themes map onto these three books for now.

## Design

Existing tokens: warm off-white, near-black ink, terracotta and deep teal; Fraunces for headings and scripture, Inter for UI, JetBrains Mono for references and metadata. Generous radius, real elevation, 150ms transitions. Mobile first, single column, max 480px, desktop is the same column centred. Dark mode finished, not partial.

No artwork is generated or bought. Until real public-domain images (Met Open Access, Rijksmuseum, Wikimedia — Doré, Rembrandt, manuscripts) are wired in, every "artwork" is drawn in CSS: a diagonal gradient, a soft radial sun bleeding off one corner, two radial hills at the bottom edge — the same shapes the funnel already uses. Never an AI-generated or stock image. The artwork record still stores artist, year and source for when real images land.

Excluded throughout: the word "AI" in any heading or button, sparkle/robot/brain icons, purple gradients, glassmorphism, typewriter animation, stock photos, second-person faith claims, promised outcomes, countdowns, fake urgency.

## Technical

- **Backend:** Lovable Cloud (Postgres + auth + storage). Tables: `plans` (the frozen record), `plan_days`, `sessions`, `session_steps`, `insights`, `verses`, `artworks`, `progress`, `notes`, `subscriptions`. Content tables are readable by any signed-in user; per-user tables are scoped to `auth.uid()` with RLS and explicit grants.
- **Auth:** magic link (OTP email link), no passwords. Account is provisioned server-side at payment from the checkout email.
- **Payments:** Stripe via Lovable's built-in payments integration, test mode first. The intro price is charged at once and the subscription renews after the intro period — no Stripe trial period is configured. Cancellation is a server function that cancels at period end, reachable in one click from the account. The webhook is the source of truth: it creates the account, freezes the plan record, and triggers the email.
- **Email:** Lovable's built-in app email — one transactional template, receipt plus sign-in link.
- **Routes:** `/plan` (My Plan) and `/plan/day/$n` (session) under an authenticated layout; `/auth` handles the magic link, the "send me a sign-in link" form, and any signed-out `/plan` visit. Reads go through TanStack Query with server functions; the session step write is a mutation so progress survives a closed tab.
- **Generation script:** a server-side script run once — imports WEB verses, then writes the 90 sessions with their insights, sourced only from the three public-domain commentators, validating every reference against the verses table before insert.

## Order of work

1. Cloud + schema + RLS.
2. Verse import (WEB) and the CSS artwork records.
3. Generate **only the first 7 days of John** — enough to build and test against.
4. Stripe checkout wired into the existing checkout screen + webhook (account, frozen plan, email), plus one-click cancel.
5. Auth: magic link, the sign-in-link form, and the authenticated layout.
6. My Plan screen, including the after-day-30 offer.
7. Session screen with resume.
8. End-to-end test-card run on a phone viewport, then the dark mode pass.
9. Only then generate the remaining 83 sessions in the background.
